Most distributors can tell you their margin. Far fewer can tell you which cost they used to work it out.

Say you bought Parle-G at ₹92 a case in March. In May the rate went to ₹98. You still have 40 cases of the old stock sitting in the godown.

Now you sell a case. What did it cost you?

If your software says ₹98, your margin looks worse than it is. If it says ₹92, it looks better than it is. The honest answer is somewhere in between — and it moves every time a new purchase lands.

That number is the weighted-average cost. Almost nobody works it out by hand, because doing it properly means recomputing it on every purchase, for every item, forever.

Dhela recalculates it on every bill it reads. And when you issue a sales invoice, the cost of that sale is locked at that moment — so the profit in your report is the profit your books will show, not an estimate that quietly drifts as rates move.

It is an unglamorous feature. It is also the difference between knowing your margin and guessing it.
